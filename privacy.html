1. Local processing of your files

All PDF, image, text, and data processing on this site happens locally in your browser, using JavaScript and other technologies built into modern browsers. When you select or drag a file into a tool, the browser reads it directly from your device into your computer's or phone's memory. The work is done there, and the result is handed back to you as a download or on-screen preview.

This means:

  • Your files, text, and data are never uploaded to our servers or to any other server.
  • We cannot view, copy, store, analyze, or share the content you process.
  • Closing or refreshing the page clears anything held in the browser's memory for that session.
  • Because nothing is sent over the network, the tools can keep working even if your internet connection drops after the page has finished loading.
Want to verify this yourself? Open your browser's developer tools, switch to the Network tab, and use any tool. You will see that no request carries your file or its contents.

2. Information we do and don't collect

What we don't collect

  • The files, images, text, or data you process with any tool
  • The names, contents, or metadata of your documents
  • Passwords, payment details, or account information (we don't offer accounts or paid checkout)

What may be collected automatically

Like most websites, the servers that deliver this site's pages may record routine technical data when you visit, such as your IP address, browser type, device type, referring page, and the pages you view. In addition, the third-party services described in section 4 may collect usage data of their own. This information describes your visit to the website. It is never derived from the contents of your files.

Information you send us voluntarily

If you contact us through the contact page, we receive the details you choose to include, such as your name, email address, and message. We use this only to read and reply to your message. Please don't attach or paste confidential documents into a support message.

3. Browser storage and cookies

Cookies are small text files that a website saves in your browser. Browser storage features such as localStorage, sessionStorage, and IndexedDB work in a similar way. Here is how they relate to this site.

Functional storage (on your device only)

Some tools may use browser storage to remember simple preferences, such as your chosen output quality, page-size setting, or interface options, so you don't have to reselect them each visit. This data stays in your browser, is never sent to us, and doesn't contain your files. Tools may also use temporary in-memory or session storage while a task is running. Your browser clears this when you close the tab or window.

Cookies from third parties

Analytics and advertising providers (see section 4) may set their own cookies or use similar technologies, such as pixels and local storage identifiers, to measure traffic and show ads. Those cookies are controlled by the providers under their own policies.

How to manage cookies and storage

You can view, block, or delete cookies and site data at any time from your browser's settings. Most browsers also offer private or incognito windows that discard this data when you close them. Blocking cookies won't stop the tools from working, although some saved preferences may not be remembered, and ads you see may be less relevant to you.

4. Third-party analytics and advertising

To understand how the site is used and to help cover the costs of keeping the tools free, we may use third-party services such as Google Analytics and Google AdSense. These services run separately from the tools themselves. They can see information about your visit to the page, but they cannot see the files you open in a tool, because those files never leave your browser.

Google Analytics

Google Analytics helps us understand aggregate usage: which pages are visited, roughly where visitors come from, which devices and browsers they use, and how long they stay. It uses cookies or similar identifiers and may collect your IP address and device information. Learn more at How Google uses information from sites that use its services. You can opt out with the Google Analytics Opt-out Browser Add-on.

Google AdSense and other advertising partners

Google, as a third-party vendor, uses cookies to serve ads on this site. Google's use of advertising cookies enables it and its partners to serve ads to you based on your visit to this site and other sites on the internet. Ads may be personalized or non-personalized depending on your location, your consent choices, and your Google account settings.

  • You can opt out of personalized advertising by visiting Google Ads Settings.
  • You can also opt out of some third-party vendors' use of cookies for personalized advertising at aboutads.info.
  • Visitors in the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom, and Switzerland may be shown a consent prompt before non-essential cookies are used, and can change their choice at any time.
Advertisements are provided by third parties. We do not sell your personal information, and we never share the contents of your files with advertisers or analytics providers, because we never receive them.

5. External CDN libraries

To keep the site fast, some tools load open-source JavaScript libraries and fonts from public Content Delivery Networks (CDNs), such as cdnjs, jsDelivr, or Google Fonts. Examples include libraries for reading and editing PDFs, compressing images, or rendering text.

When your browser fetches one of these libraries, the CDN receives a standard web request. That request normally includes your IP address, browser and device details, and the address of the page requesting the library. This is how all web content delivery works, and CDN operators handle that data under their own privacy policies.

  • CDNs only deliver the library code to your browser. They do not receive your files or the content you process.
  • The libraries then run locally on your device, the same way the rest of the site does.
  • CDN providers may keep short-term server logs for security and performance reasons.

If you'd rather not contact these services, you can block them in your browser or with a content blocker, but the tools that depend on them may not work.

6. Children's privacy

This website is a general-purpose utility and is not directed to children under 13. We do not knowingly collect personal information from children. If you believe a child has sent us personal information, please contact us and we will delete it.

7. Your choices and rights

Depending on where you live, you may have rights under laws such as the GDPR (European Economic Area and United Kingdom), the CCPA/CPRA (California), or similar data protection laws. These may include the right to access, correct, delete, or object to the processing of personal data that a service holds about you.

Because your files never reach us, there is no file data for us to access, correct, or delete. For the limited data that may be collected through analytics, advertising, or messages sent to us, you can:

  • Manage or delete cookies and site data in your browser settings
  • Opt out of personalized advertising using the links in section 4
  • Use browser features or extensions that limit tracking, such as Do Not Track or Global Privacy Control signals
  • Ask us about, or request deletion of, any message you've sent us through the contact page
